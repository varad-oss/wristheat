## Q1. What does the problem map look like for this lens, and where is the evidence strongest?

### Takeaway

The lens has one recurring failure: **systems measure filings, closures, plantings and audit reports, not durable outcomes.** Complaints get marked "resolved" without being fixed, and saplings get counted but not kept alive. Rainwater harvesting (RWH) pits are installed to avoid a penalty but never checked, and audits are filed for accreditation without proof that anything was saved.

The evidence is strongest for four of these:
- **Complaint loops:** false closures and recurring dumping spots.
- **Plantation survival:** about 33% survival in one state audit.
- **RWH:** compliance only on paper, with about 18% coverage in Bengaluru.
- **Heat-vulnerable elderly:** India has no equivalent of France's heat registry.

New rules keep creating obligations with no tooling for the people who must meet them. These include the SWM Rules 2026 duties for events and bulk generators, the DPCC dust self-audit every fortnight, the November 2025 GRAP revision putting offices at 50% from Stage III, and NAAC audit requirements.

Student civic-complaint apps are already a crowded genre (MumbaiHacks 2025, SIH 2025). Any complaint concept therefore needs a different mechanism, such as verifying closures or adjusting for rainfall, not routing plus SLAs.

### Inferences

- **Design rule for this lens (follows from P12 and constraint D).** The product must deliver value to the *person who acts* (an RWA volunteer, organiser, eco-club student or reporter) even if the institution never responds. Concepts whose value depends on a ward office answering are demo-able only as fixtures and are weak.
- **The "reporting vs outcome" gap is the lens's best differentiator.** The strongest concepts add a **verification loop** that existing tools skip:
  - re-checking closed complaints (C-02)
  - sapling survival (C-07)
  - weather-normalised energy savings (C-12)
  - re-measured after-hours load (C-11)
  - reconciling event waste against the plan (C-10)
- **AWS Open Data is unusually useful here.** The canopy-height data, Overture buildings and NASA POWER give Built-on-AWS stories that are *load-bearing*: query in place with Athena, read Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFFs from S3. This beats decorative services.
- **Step Functions fits coordination concepts naturally.** Escalation ladders, check-in timeouts with `waitForTaskToken`, and fan-out of tasks after a GRAP stage change all show as an execution graph in the video. That directly serves the rulebook's R2.
- **No SMS in India without DLT.** Any concept that relies on SMS or voice notifications must use email, web push or Telegram in the MVP and say so.

### Gaps

- No per-attendee waste-generation coefficients for Indian events were found. C-10 would launch with editable defaults labelled as assumptions.
- No Indian figure on after-hours energy share in schools or colleges; the US figure is a proxy.
- No data on minimum pickup quantities for authorised e-waste recyclers (affects C-13's threshold).
- Columns, cities and status fields in the IChangeMyCity CSV are unverified. Download it before committing to C-02's back-test.
- Open-Meteo hourly rainfall is coarse (9–25 km) and was not live-tested. No open sub-hourly municipal rain-gauge API was verified (Bengaluru KSNDMC and Mumbai MCGM gauges are unchecked). This is the main risk for C-09.
- Building heights for India in Overture are unverified (affects C-06's shadow casting).
- The SWM Rules 2026 gazette text on events was seen only through Kerala's directive, not the gazette itself.
- No source found on how many Indian RWAs run any heat-vulnerable check-in. The absence is inferred.

#### C-01 · Right Desk: an escalation clock for burning and dust complaints (Air)

1. **Actual problem:** Citizens report garbage burning or construction dust once, through whichever app they know. The complaint may go to the wrong authority, carries no evidence, and when it stalls nothing escalates.
   - Green Delhi left 8,599 MCD complaints pending in 2024 (FACT, [DH](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/delhi/delhi-govt-receives-over-84000-pollution-complaints-through-green-delhi-app-this-year-3331687)).
   - A Swachhata complaint was shown "resolved" without action (FACT, [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/jalandhar/swachhata-app-loses-favour-with-jalandhar-residents-714798)).
2. **Primary user:** A resident-activist or RWA environment volunteer in an NCR or other North Indian city in winter.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - Open burning is up to 3× higher in winter and concentrated in poorer neighbourhoods (FACT, [WRI via CivilsDaily](https://www.civilsdaily.com/news/pollution-from-open-waste-burning-triples-in-cities-during-winter-study/)).
   - CAQM found "persistent" open burning in Dec 2025 (FACT, [TeamLease](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50949/caqm-comprehensive-review-of-municipal-solid-waste-msw-management-across-the-ncr-states/)).
   - Its exposure impact is local, not citywide: under 1% of city PM (FACT, same WRI summary).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Green Delhi app, CAQM's SAMEER complaint function and Swachhata (KNOWN).
   - Tagging officials on X (KNOWN).
   - Student apps such as MumbaiHacks' Civic Lens already route complaints and auto-escalate on the authority side (FACT, [Devfolio](https://devfolio.co/projects/crowdsourced-civic-lssue-reporting-and-resolution-system-7cc8)).
   - **The gap:** no citizen-side tool that covers all channels, packages statutory evidence and times the escalations (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** An escalation is only credible when it cites the specific rule, the specific deadline and that deadline's lapse. Citizens don't know any of the three (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Capture:** a photo with EXIF time and GPS. Bedrock vision classifies it as burning, dust or dumping.
   - **Route:** point-in-polygon against ward and ULB boundaries resolves the authority, channel and legal hook.
   - **Evidence pack:** the hourly wind direction from Open-Meteo shows the plume's heading and any school or hospital within 300 m downwind (OSM).
   - **Escalate:** a Step Functions ladder waits for each tier's SLA, asks the user "resolved?", and drafts the next-tier appeal if not.
7. **What's distinctive:** Statutory routing and timing, done on the citizen's side, plus an evidence pack with downwind receptors.
8. **Not just a new UI?** Partly it is. Escalation on SLA exists on the authority side in student apps (FACT, Devfolio above). The difference is that it is citizen-side and covers several channels (INFERENCE).
9. **Data:**
   - Ward boundary GeoJSON from OpenCity/DataMeet (KNOWN; not verified this session).
   - Open-Meteo wind (03: forecast API works without a key).
   - OSM Overpass (KNOWN public).
   - A channel and SLA table built by hand from public sources: 12 h for garbage dumps (FACT, Tribune above).
10. **Architecture:** PWA → API Gateway → Lambda (classify, route). Plus S3 for evidence, DynamoDB for cases, Step Functions for the ladder, Bedrock for vision and drafting, SES to email drafts to the user.
11. **AWS use:** Step Functions Wait states are the product's logic and are visible. Bedrock does photo classification and bilingual drafting.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One city (Delhi). Three violation types. A three-tier ladder. An evidence PDF.
13. **Demo plan:** A team member photographs a safe, staged smouldering leaf pile or uses a labelled stock photo. The case is filed to a *mock* inbox. The SLA is compressed to 2 minutes, and the ladder fires on camera. No real authority is involved; this is labelled.
14. **Strongest video moment:** The Step Functions graph turns red at "SLA lapsed" and drops a ready-to-send tier-2 appeal that cites the rule and the timestamped evidence.
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The 84% "resolved" figure is self-reported (FACT, DH).
    - That escalation raises real resolution rates is HYPOTHESIS; no evidence was found.
    - Environmental benefit: speculative.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** ward polygons go stale; there is no API to file into government apps (copy and paste only).
    - **Product:** officials ignore escalations.
    - **Competition:** high. A crowded genre.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "Report pollution" appears in every generic prompt (05, §F). The SIH and MumbaiHacks precedents exist.
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Statute-timed escalation with downwind-receptor evidence. That is still thin; **merge into C-02 or drop.**

#### C-02 · ReCheck: did the fix last? A survival curve for complaint closures (Waste & Energy)

1. **Actual problem:** Garbage dumps and GVPs get "closed" by staff with a photo, then reappear. No one measures how long a fix lasts.
   - Fake closure photos in Gurugram (FACT, [HT](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-chandigarh/20180714/281629601041926)).
   - 1,109 GVPs reappeared after being "eliminated" in Hyderabad (FACT, [DC](https://www.pressreader.com/india/deccan-chronicle/20180626/281663960740956)).
2. **Primary user:** A neighbourhood volunteer group, RWA or citizen collective (e.g., Janaagraha-style city groups) that wants ward-level accountability.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - Recurring spots feed open burning (CAQM directed GVP surveillance; FACT, [TeamLease](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/50949/caqm-comprehensive-review-of-municipal-solid-waste-msw-management-across-the-ncr-states/)).
   - Self-reported closure rates such as MCD's 84% are uncheckable (FACT, [DH](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/delhi/delhi-govt-receives-over-84000-pollution-complaints-through-green-delhi-app-this-year-3331687)).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Re-filing the same complaint: 38 re-opened in Jalandhar (FACT, [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/archive/jalandhar/swachhata-app-loses-favour-with-jalandhar-residents-714798)).
   - Authority-side proof-of-resolution photos (FACT, Devfolio Civic Lens). These are taken *at closure*, which is exactly what Gurugram faked.
   - None of the apps re-visits a spot later (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** Closure is a single event; *cleanliness is a duration*. Measuring the time until a spot reverts turns anecdotes into a metric that is hard to game (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Fingerprint the spot:** GPS within ±15 m plus a reference photo.
   - **Schedule re-checks:** when the user marks it officially resolved, EventBridge Scheduler prompts the reporter or any nearby watcher at +24 h, +72 h, +7 d and +30 d.
   - **Judge each re-check:** Bedrock vision answers "same place? garbage present?" with a confidence score.
   - **Compute the curve:** a Kaplan–Meier "clean-survival" curve per spot and per ward, giving a **resolution half-life**.
   - **Escalate patterns:** a spot that reverts twice within 30 days becomes a "structural GVP" with a fix category (missing bin, collection-time mismatch, C&D debris). A monthly ward scorecard follows.
   - **Back-test:** run the curve on the IChangeMyCity 2019–22 log, if its columns permit.
7. **What's distinctive:** It measures how *durable* a resolution is, using survival analysis on crowd re-verification. No authority-side app does this (INFERENCE).
8. **Not just a new UI?** The output is a new metric (half-life) that existing systems don't compute. It needs scheduled re-observation, not just display.
9. **Data:**
   - User photos.
   - The IChangeMyCity CSV, CC BY-SA 2.0, 7.9 MiB (FACT, [OpenCity](https://data.opencity.in/dataset/i-change-my-city-data/resource/i-change-my-city-complaints-log---2019---2022)); its fields are not verified.
   - The AWS Registry copy is deprecated (FACT, [Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/ichangemycity/)).
   - Ward polygons (KNOWN).
10. **Architecture:** PWA → API Gateway → Lambda. S3 for photos; DynamoDB for spots and observations; EventBridge Scheduler for re-checks; Bedrock vision for comparison; Athena over the ICMyC CSV in S3 for the historical curves.
11. **AWS use:** EventBridge Scheduler is the re-check engine. Bedrock does the before/after judgement. Athena turns the open complaint log into survival curves.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** Report, close, and three re-checks with one survival curve. Plus one historical chart from the ICMyC data.
13. **Demo plan:**
    - Teammates photograph two real litter spots near campus (no authority involved) and simulate a "resolved" flag.
    - Re-checks are time-compressed and labelled.
    - The historical curve uses real open data.
    - There is no dependency on any ULB.
14. **Strongest video moment:** A spot marked "Resolved ✅ by the corporation" shows a 72-hour re-check photo with the garbage back. The ward's half-life chart reads "median clean life: 3.1 days" (illustrative and labelled).
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - Recurrence is documented (FACT, Hyderabad, Gurugram).
    - That a public half-life metric changes ward behaviour is HYPOTHESIS.
    - Diverting waste from burning is speculative.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** the ICMyC columns may lack closure timestamps; image matching under varied angles.
    - **Product:** re-check fatigue among volunteers.
    - **Competition:** medium. Complaint apps are crowded, but verification is rare.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "Proof of resolution" is a known feature (Civic Lens), so teams may add "citizen confirms".
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Multi-interval re-observation with a survival curve and half-life metric, back-tested on real Janaagraha data.

#### C-03 · Half-Office: a GRAP Stage III roster that minimises commute emissions (Air)

1. **Actual problem:** NCR offices must run at 50% on site under GRAP Stage III. HR picks who comes in arbitrarily, by rotation or alphabet, ignoring who drives 40 km alone.
   - The 50% rule moved to Stage III on 22 Nov 2025 (FACT, [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/11/22/delhi-air-pollution-work-from-home-and-public-private-offices-to-work-at-50-per-cent-strength-during-grap-iii.amp.html)).
   - Delhi ordered public and private offices to 50% on 24 Nov 2025 (FACT, [DH](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fdelhi%2Fdelhi-pollution-all-private-government-offices-ordered-to-operate-at-50-capacity-on-site-work-from-home-for-the-rest-3809360)).
2. **Primary user:** The HR or admin lead at a 50–2,000-person NCR office or college administration.
3. **Why it matters:** Vehicle traffic is a target of GRAP. The order's purpose is fewer commutes (INFERENCE). Which half stays home determines the vehicle-km avoided (INFERENCE).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:** Spreadsheets, team-wise rotation and HRMS hybrid-roster features (KNOWN, e.g., desk-booking tools). None optimises for emissions or exposure (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** The 50% constraint is fixed, but the *choice* of people is a free variable with a large spread in vehicle-km. Solo long-distance drivers matter far more than metro users (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Inputs:** an employee CSV with home location (pincode or lat/lon), mode, team and critical role.
   - **Distances:** an Amazon Location route matrix.
   - **Optimisation:** an integer linear programme. Each day ≤50% are on site; each team has its minimum coverage; critical roles are present. Minimise Σ(vehicle-km × mode emission factor) + λ·(commute minutes × forecast AQI).
   - **Outputs:** a weekly roster, a compliance notice drafted by Bedrock, and a comparison against a rotation roster.
7. **What's distinctive:** It turns a regulatory cap into an optimisation that halves the *right* commutes.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It needs a constrained optimiser and a route matrix, not just a display.
9. **Data:**
   - Employee CSV (user-provided; synthetic for the demo).
   - Amazon Location (AWS).
   - CAMS AQI forecast via Open-Meteo (03).
   - Mode emission factors: the India GHG Program or ARAI (KNOWN; **not verified, gap**).
10. **Architecture:** Amplify web → Lambda in a container (OR-Tools/PuLP) → Location `CalculateRouteMatrix` → DynamoDB → Bedrock (notice) → SES.
11. **AWS use:** Location Service is load-bearing for distances. A Lambda container runs the solver. EventBridge re-solves on a stage change.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** Upload 60 employees, solve, and show the roster plus "vehicle-km avoided vs rotation".
13. **Demo plan:** A synthetic 60-person company, labelled, using real NCR geography and real route distances. No real employer is needed.
14. **Strongest video moment:** A side-by-side: the rotation roster avoids 1,100 vehicle-km a week and the optimised one avoids 2,400 (numbers illustrative). The map's red long-commute lines vanish.
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The policy exists (FACT).
    - The size of the emission spread is INFERENCE.
    - Real uptake by HR is HYPOTHESIS.
    - Benefit only on Stage III days (a few weeks a year) is INFERENCE.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** low.
    - **Product:** NCR only and seasonal (D is partial); fairness complaints from employees.
    - **Competition:** low.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** GRAP is salient in Delhi in October and Air will be crowded (05).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Optimisation over route-matrix distances with team-coverage constraints and an auditable "km avoided" figure.

5. **Underlying insight:** GRAP is effectively a runbook. What institutions lack is the *diff*: which of their assets each stage change affects, who owns each action, and proof that it was done (INFERENCE).

9. **Data:**

10. **Architecture:** Amplify → API Gateway → Lambda. Step Functions (a Map state per task). DynamoDB, S3 (proof), Cognito (owners), SES.
11. **AWS use:** The Step Functions fan-out and callbacks are visible. Cognito handles roles.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One campus fixture and the Stage II→III transition: 8 tasks, 4 owners, proof uploads.
13. **Demo plan:** A fictional campus (labelled). Teammates act as owners closing tasks on phones.
14. **Strongest video moment:** The stage flips to III, and 8 tasks fan out live across 4 phones. The compliance meter fills to 100% with timestamped proof.
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - Obligations exist (FACT).
    - Faster, more complete compliance is HYPOTHESIS.

16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** encoding GRAP accurately.
    - **Product:** NCR only (D partial); "workflow tool" fatigue.
    - **Competition:** medium. GRAP-Ready appeared in 07 and school AQI alerts are crowded.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "School safety on bad days" is suggested verbatim by the organisers (03/05).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** A stage diff by asset type, with proof-of-completion and a forecast pre-warning.

#### C-05 · Heat Roll-Call: a vulnerable-neighbour check-in that escalates (Heat & Water)

1. **Actual problem:** On heatwave days, older people who live alone are at highest risk and nobody is assigned to check on them.
   - Over-65 heat deaths rose 55% (FACT, [DTE 2025](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/climate-change/protecting-indias-geriatric-population-from-the-rising-heat-burden)).
   - HAPs lack outreach for older people (FACT, [DTE 2026](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/climate-change/indias-elderly-face-deadly-toll-as-heatwaves-intensify)).
2. **Primary user:** An RWA, a mohalla volunteer group, an old-age-care NGO or a hostel warden (for students with medical conditions). The roster coordinator is the user; volunteers are participants.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - India's heat illness is badly undercounted. There were 7,192 suspected heatstroke cases from 1 Mar to 24 Jun 2025 (FACT, via 03: [The Week/PTI](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/07/27/del4-climate-heat-rti.html)), and experts say surveillance captures only a fraction (FACT, via 03).
   - Older people were 10.5% of the population in 2022 (FACT, DTE 2025).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - IMD, SACHET and NDMA alerts go to individuals (KNOWN; 04).
   - Family WhatsApp groups.
   - Ahmedabad trains ASHA workers (FACT, DTE 2026).
   - **The international model is France's** CCAS registry with daily calls and police follow-up (FACT, [Lyon](https://www.lyon.fr/demarche/personnes-agees/inscription-sur-le-fichier-canicule)).
   - No Indian community tool was found (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** An alert reaches the vulnerable person. What saves them is a *named other person* who is obliged to check and an *escalation path when that person doesn't*. This is a coordination problem, not an information problem (INFERENCE, consistent with the French design).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Registry:** residents are registered with consent: age, lives alone, conditions, fan or cooler, buddy, backup, emergency contact.
   - **Trigger:** EventBridge Scheduler checks the Open-Meteo Tmax and apparent-temperature forecast daily against a city threshold (configurable; IMD criteria are the default).
   - **Check-ins:** on an alert day, Step Functions starts one execution per person. At 11:00 the buddy gets a task with a Bedrock-generated Hindi or English script and a Polly voice note.
   - **Escalation:** each step waits with `waitForTaskToken` for a timeout. A "no answer" or a timeout escalates to the backup, then the RWA secretary, then the emergency contact.
   - **Board:** a live status board shows ✅ OK, ⚠ needs help or ❌ unreachable. The day-after summary lists cooling and ORS actions taken.
7. **What's distinctive:** It is the first Indian community-scale adaptation of the fichier-canicule pattern (INFERENCE). Escalation is coded as a state machine with timeouts, not as an alert.
8. **Not just a new UI?** Alerts already exist. This is an **obligation and escalation protocol** among people, which no Indian alert app implements (INFERENCE).
9. **Data:**
   - Open-Meteo forecast for Tmax and apparent temperature (03: no key).
   - City HAP thresholds (public PDFs; KNOWN).
   - The registry (user-provided).
   - **SMS is blocked by DLT** (FACT, [AWS docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/sms-voice/latest/userguide/registrations-sms-senderid-india.md)), so notifications use web push, a Telegram bot or SES.
10. **Architecture:** PWA plus Telegram bot → API Gateway → Lambda. EventBridge Scheduler (daily forecast check), Step Functions (one execution per vulnerable person, with callback tokens), DynamoDB, Bedrock (scripts), Polly (Hindi voice), Cognito (volunteers).
11. **AWS use:** Step Functions `waitForTaskToken` with timeouts *is* the escalation logic and shows clearly in the console. EventBridge supplies the trigger. Polly provides local-language audio.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One RWA, 6 residents, 3 volunteers and one heat day, with escalation end to end.
13. **Demo plan:**
    - A real Open-Meteo forecast for a hot city. If October is not hot, use a labelled **replay of a real May 2024 heat day** from Open-Meteo history.
    - Residents are synthetic (labelled).
    - Three teammates act as buddies on real phones; one deliberately doesn't answer.
    - No outside cooperation is needed.
14. **Strongest video moment:** "Mrs Sharma: buddy didn't confirm in 2 h → escalated to backup → backup taps 'needs help' → RWA secretary gets ORS and cooling-room instructions." All of it is visible in the Step Functions graph.
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - France's system exists (FACT, Lyon).
    - The mortality benefit of buddy systems was not researched here (gap).
    - Lives saved: speculative. The demonstrable outcome is "100% of the registered vulnerable contacted within X h".
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** notification channels (no SMS).
    - **Product:** DPDP Act consent and health-data sensitivity (KNOWN); seasonal (Mar–Jun); October demo relevance, so use the replay.
    - **Competition:** low (heat alert bots are crowded, but check-in rosters are not).
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** Heatwave-alert bots are a crowded prompt result (05: 6/100). A team might add "notify family".
18. **What would distinguish ours:** A registry, named buddies and a timed escalation ladder, modelled on an existing national protocol, with a measurable contact-coverage KPI.

#### C-06 · ShadeSite: plant where it shades the most people (Heat & Water)

1. **Actual problem:** Plantation drives at schools, colleges, RWAs and municipalities pick spots for convenience: an empty corner or the campus boundary. They don't target the paths people walk at 1–4 pm in May.
   - 10.35 crore saplings were planted in one school drive (FACT, [DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/Ek_Ped_Maa_Ke_naam_2-0.pdf)).
   - Siting guidance was not found (gap).
2. **Primary user:** A school or college eco-club coordinator, an RWA horticulture committee, or a ward or CSR plantation organiser.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - Tree shade cuts pedestrian heat stress substantially. Parasols achieve about 56% of a street tree's UTCI reduction (FACT, [ICUC12](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/ICUC12/ICUC12-703.html)).
   - HAPs are not localised (FACT, [CPR](https://cprindia.org/briefsreports/how-is-india-adapting-to-heatwaves-an-assessment-of-heat-action-plans-with-insights-for-transformative-climate-action/)).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - The Eco Clubs portal logs plantings (FACT, DoSEL).
   - Tree-census apps and i-Tree (KNOWN; benefit estimates, not shade siting).
   - Generic GIS such as QGIS needs expertise.
   - **Gap:** none ranks candidate spots by shade delivered to walkers at peak heat (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** A tree's heat value depends on *where its shadow falls at 2 pm in May* and *how many people walk there*. That is computable from canopy height, building footprints, sun position and footpaths (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Layers:** the user draws a polygon or picks a school. The system pulls WRI/Meta canopy-height tiles for existing trees, Overture or OSM buildings (heights assumed where missing), and OSM footways, school gates and bus stops as demand weights.
   - **Current shade:** sun positions for 11:00–16:00 in April–June (pvlib) drive shadow casting on a 2 m grid, giving the shaded fraction of each path segment.
   - **Candidates:** open-ground spots are each simulated as a mature crown (species diameter and height).
   - **Selection:** a greedy pick of N saplings by **marginal shade-minutes on weighted paths**.
   - **Output:** a planting map, a species list (curated native list) and a predicted shade-minute gain.
7. **What's distinctive:** It optimises the *heat benefit per sapling*, not canopy cover, using AWS Open Data canopy heights.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It is geometric shadow simulation plus optimisation, with no display-only layer.
9. **Data:**
   - WRI/Meta canopy height on S3 (**verified accessible**; CC BY 4.0; [Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/dataforgood-fb-forests/)).
   - Overture buildings on S3 ([Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/overture/)). Indian heights are unverified (gap).
   - OSM Overpass (KNOWN).
   - pvlib sun position (OSS).
10. **Architecture:** Amplify (MapLibre) → API Gateway → a Lambda container (rasterio reads Cloud-Optimised GeoTIFF windows from S3; shapely and numpy run the shadow casting) → DynamoDB (plans) → Bedrock (plain-language planting brief).
11. **AWS use:** It reads AWS Open Data in place, and Athena or DuckDB query Overture parquet on S3. The video can show the S3 paths and Athena query. Lambda does the computing.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One campus and one month-hour window, ranking the top 10 spots with a shade-minutes metric.
13. **Demo plan:** A real public campus (e.g., DTU or IIT Delhi), using public data only. The output is advisory. No institution needs to agree.
14. **Strongest video moment:** A time-lapse slider over the 1–4 pm May sun. The current walkway is mostly red (unshaded). Ten pins drop, and the walkway turns 62% green (illustrative), with "1 sapling here = 41 shade-minutes for 300 walkers/day."
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The physics of shade is solid (FACT, ICUC12).
    - Shade-minutes are modelled (INFERENCE).
    - Saplings take years to cast shade, so the benefit is deferred and survival-dependent (FACT, [CAG 33.51%](https://cag.gov.in/uploads/download_audit_report/2024/9_Chapter-5_IoCAA-067b7207e116ad2.51862799.pdf)).
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** GIS effort in 48 h, missing building heights, raster vintage and alignment.
    - **Product:** benefit is far in the future.
    - **Competition:** low.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "Urban heat island map" from Landsat LST is a known idea (07 I-20). A team might do "where to plant" by land surface temperature (LST).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Ranking spots by pedestrian-weighted, sun-path shade-minutes rather than LST hotspots, built on AWS-hosted canopy heights. **Pair with C-07** for survival.

#### C-08 · Monsoon Replay: size a shared rain-harvest by replaying real past monsoons (Heat & Water)

1. **Actual problem:** RWAs and plot owners can't decide what RWH to build: tank or recharge, what size, who pays. Mandates then yield pits built only to avoid penalties.
   - Bengaluru is at ~18% compliance and BWSSB does not check function (FACT, [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/bengaluru-rainwater-harvesting-bwssb-kscst-water/)).
   - Hyderabad has ~17,000 non-compliant buildings (FACT, [Realty Today](https://therealtytoday.com/news/over-17000-buildings-in-hyderabad-to-face-penalties-for-non-compliance-with-rainwater-harvesting-rules)).
2. **Primary user:** An RWA committee member preparing an AGM proposal, or the owner of a plot in a city with an RWH mandate.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - 730 groundwater units are over-exploited (FACT, via 03: [ETV Bharat](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/bharat/nearly-11-per-cent-groundwater-assessment-units-over-exploited-govt-enn26072007475)).
   - Tanker prices spike (FACT, via 03).
   - The SV Symphony case: ₹1.6 lakh spent, more than ₹3 lakh a year saved, ~2,400 tankers avoided (FACT, [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/bengaluru-rainwater-harvesting-3-lakh-annual-savings-borewell-recharge-37514)).
   - Roof runoff also feeds street waterlogging (INFERENCE).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Consultants and plumbers.
   - Simple calculators of annual rainfall × area × coefficient (KNOWN, e.g., CGWB/CSE-style). These ignore storage overflow on heavy days (INFERENCE).
   - BWSSB penalties (FACT).
   - **None shows a society how much of last year's actual monsoon its tank would have caught** (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** Indian monsoon rain falls in bursts, so tank size, not roof area, limits capture. The simple formula overstates capture and hides the optimal size. A daily water-balance replay of *real* past monsoons gives a trustworthy, locally specific answer people can see (INFERENCE; the yield-after-spillage method is standard in RWH engineering, KNOWN).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Roofs:** pick a society or street on a map. Overture building footprints come from an Athena query on S3; the user confirms roof types (runoff coefficient).

   - **Outputs:**
     - a capture-vs-size curve with its "knee"
     - litres captured and recharged per year
     - **tanker-equivalents** and ₹ at a user-entered local tanker rate
     - a city mandate check (BWSSB, HMWSSB tables)
     - per-flat cost split and a one-page AGM pack drafted by Bedrock
7. **What's distinctive:** A replay of real past monsoons with storage dynamics, aggregated across neighbouring roofs into one shared system.
8. **Not just a new UI?** A simulation engine on real time series replaces the static formula.
9. **Data:**
   - Overture buildings on S3 ([Registry](https://registry.opendata.aws/overture/)).
   - Open-Meteo Historical: ERA5 at 0.25°, daily or hourly ([docs](https://open-meteo.com/en/docs/historical-weather-api)); live call not verified (gap). Fallback: IMD gridded rainfall via the `imdlib` library (KNOWN; not verified).
   - Tanker price: user input.
10. **Architecture:** Amplify (map) → API Gateway → Lambda. Athena on Overture parquet; Lambda (numpy simulation); S3 (cached rain); Bedrock (AGM pack); DynamoDB.
11. **AWS use:** Athena queries AWS Open Data in place to fetch footprints, which is visible in the console. Lambda runs the simulation.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One society polygon, 5 monsoons, 3 tank sizes plus recharge, and one result card.
13. **Demo plan:** A real Bengaluru apartment complex footprint with real rainfall history. Only the inputs (tanker ₹, roof type) are labelled assumptions. No RWA needs to cooperate.
14. **Strongest video moment:** The 2024 monsoon replays as an animation. The tank fills and overflows on burst days, and a counter reads "your roofs sent 3.1 million litres to the drain = 517 tankers = ₹6.2 lakh". The capture curve's knee then says "a 40 kL tank catches 71%; 100 kL catches only 78%" (illustrative and labelled).
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The SV Symphony outcome is FACT (one case).
    - Savings for the user's society are modelled.
    - That groundwater recharge raises borewell yields is anecdotal in the source (FACT, as anecdote).
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** ERA5 at 0.25° smooths rainfall extremes and so understates bursts (INFERENCE); Overture coverage gaps; first-flush and filter losses.

    - **Competition:** medium (RWH calculators exist).
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "Rainwater harvesting calculator" is an obvious water-scarcity idea (INFERENCE).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** A replay of real monsoons with tank dynamics, multi-roof aggregation via Athena on Overture, and tanker-equivalent economics in one AGM-ready output.

#### C-09 · Rain-Check: was it "unprecedented rain" or a blocked drain? (Heat & Water)

1. **Actual problem:** The same spots flood every monsoon, and agencies blame extreme rain.
   - Delhi has 500+ waterlogging points (FACT, [The Week](https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/national/2025/07/30/des120-dl-cm-ld-waterlogging.html)).
   - The CAG found Bengaluru desilting done during the monsoon and less than half the network under maintenance contracts (FACT, [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/cag-report-storm-water-drain-design-maintenance-issues/)).
2. **Primary user:** A residents' collective or ward-level civic group; secondarily, a councillor.
3. **Why it matters:** Waterlogging disrupts mobility and damages property (FACT, via 03). There is no evidence base separating maintenance failure from rain beyond design capacity (INFERENCE).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Complaint helplines and apps (KNOWN).
   - Flood-alert apps are crowded (05: 9/100).
   - Google Flood Hub is riverine (KNOWN).
   - **None normalises a waterlogging event by the rainfall that caused it** (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** A spot that floods at 15 mm/h is a maintenance failure. One that floods only above 50 mm/h is a capacity issue. Attaching rainfall intensity to each crowd report turns "it flooded again" into a falsifiable accountability claim (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Report:** a crowd report gives photo, time and depth class (ankle, knee, kerb, judged against a reference object).
   - **Rain:** hourly precipitation for the 1–3 h before the event, from Open-Meteo IFS at 9 km.
   - **Onset curve:** per spot, the minimum intensity that triggers flooding, compared with the design norm (e.g., 25 mm/h for Mumbai drains; FACT via 03).
   - **Classify each event:** *within design* means maintenance failure; *beyond design* means a capacity gap.
   - **Track** whether the onset threshold rises after the claimed desilting.
   - **Output:** a ward dossier.
7. **What's distinctive:** Rainfall-normalised attribution of waterlogging.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It is a new derived metric (the onset threshold) from joining crowd and weather time series.
9. **Data:**
   - Crowd reports (user input).
   - Open-Meteo hourly rainfall (resolution 9–25 km; **live call not verified**).
   - Municipal rain gauges (KSNDMC, MCGM): **not verified**.
   - The CAG report for context.
10. **Architecture:** PWA → Lambda → DynamoDB. Lambda fetches the weather; S3; Location Service maps; Bedrock writes the dossier.
11. **AWS use:** Lambda/DynamoDB pipeline and Bedrock (narrative). Moderate. No AWS service is uniquely load-bearing (INFERENCE).
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** 3 spots and 6 historical events, with an onset chart and dossier.
13. **Demo plan:** Real rainfall from past Bengaluru storms paired with **synthetic, labelled** reports. No authority involved.
14. **Strongest video moment:** A scatter of rain intensity against flooding events. The spot floods at 12 mm/h, below the dashed design line, stamped "maintenance failure ×4 this season".
15. **Expected-impact evidence:** The pattern of recurring spots is FACT. Accountability leading to fixes is HYPOTHESIS.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** **rainfall resolution**; convective cells are often smaller than 9 km (INFERENCE). This undermines the core claim.
    - **Product:** needs real reports across several events (it is a seasonal product).
    - **Competition:** the flood genre is crowded, but this mechanism is rare.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** Flood and waterlogging crowd maps are very common (05).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Rainfall-normalised onset thresholds. It is viable only if a fine-resolution rain source is confirmed. **Verdict: do not choose unless gauge data is verified.**

#### C-10 · FestLoop: plan event waste, then reconcile it (Waste & Energy)

1. **Actual problem:** College fests, weddings, runs and conferences have legal segregation duties, plus advance notice to the local body when over 100 people (FACT, [Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/news/kerala/2026/06/05/kerala-rolls-out-four-category-waste-management-system.amp.html); [CAG Chennai summary](https://www.cag.org.in/sites/default/files/database/report_swm_rules_2016_salient_features_20170707_0.pdf)). Organisers improvise: too few bins, no vendor booked, mixed waste to landfill (INFERENCE).
2. **Primary user:** A student fest or hackathon organising committee. Secondarily, wedding planners and run organisers.
3. **Why it matters:**
   - Event waste is concentrated and predictable, so it is avoidable.
   - BBMP's hall rules show the regulatory direction (FACT, [DTE](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/amp/story/waste/bengaluru-moves-towards-plastic-free-weddings-61620)).
   - The Bengaluru Marathon case shows what a planned approach can achieve (FACT, [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/articles/lessons-in-waste-management-from-the-bengaluru-marathon-2014)).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Specialist zero-waste NGOs, e.g., Hasiru Dala, Waste Warriors (FACT, same article).
   - "Green event" checklists (KNOWN).
   - None quantifies streams from the menu and headcount, or reconciles afterwards (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** Event waste is close to deterministic: headcount × meals × serveware. A plan that predicts kg by stream lets organisers size bins and vendors and pick serveware *before* the event. Weighing afterwards turns the plan into a verified diversion rate (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Inputs:** attendance by day, menu items, serveware (disposable or steel/plate bank), water (bottles or refill) and decor.
   - **Coefficient model:** kg per stream across the 4 SWM streams. The defaults are editable and labelled as assumptions.
   - **What-if:** switching to a plate bank and refill shows kg avoided.
   - **Plan:** bin stations, volunteer roster, vendor list (composter, recycler, waste-picker organisation) and the **local-body intimation letter**.
   - **Afterwards:** stream weights are entered, a photo of the scale display is read by Bedrock, and the system reconciles plan against actual, gives the diversion rate, issues a certificate and writes lessons into next year's coefficients.
7. **What's distinctive:** Prediction, then logistics, then measured reconciliation. Each event's actuals recalibrate the model.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It contains a quantitative model and a feedback loop, not just a checklist.
9. **Data:**
   - User inputs.
   - The rules (FACT above).
   - **Coefficients for Indian events were not found (gap).** Start from the user's last event, or from literature to be sourced (the Bengaluru Marathon's 125 kg of cups is one data point).
10. **Architecture:** Amplify → API Gateway → Lambda (model). DynamoDB, Bedrock (menu parsing, letter, scale-photo reading), S3 (certificate PDF), SES.
11. **AWS use:** Bedrock reads unstructured menus and scale photos into the deterministic model. Lambda and DynamoDB handle the rest. Moderate.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One event: plan, what-if, intimation letter, post-event entry and reconciliation.
13. **Demo plan:**
    - Plan a 2,000-person college fest as a fixture.
    - Reconcile a **real small event the team runs**, e.g., a team lunch where the bins are actually weighed, labelled.
    - No outside cooperation.
14. **Strongest video moment:** The what-if toggle "disposable → plate bank" drops predicted landfill waste from 410 kg to 95 kg (illustrative). Then the post-event card: "planned 78% diversion, achieved 71%".
15. **Expected-impact evidence:** Legal duties are FACT. Diversion gains are HYPOTHESIS until reconciled events accumulate.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** no source for the coefficients.
    - **Product:** organisers may skip the post-event weigh-in.
    - **Competition:** medium-low (food-waste apps are crowded; event planning is not).
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** Students organise fests and know the pain.
18. **What would distinguish ours:** The SWM-rule letter, a stream-level model and plan-vs-actual reconciliation that self-calibrates.

#### C-11 · NightLoad: two meter photos expose after-hours waste (Waste & Energy; education)

1. **Actual problem:** Schools, hostels, coaching centres and offices burn power overnight and at weekends (ACs, computers, pumps, corridor lights). Nobody knows how much because bills are monthly.
   - Campus buildings use up to 50% of peak demand when unoccupied (FACT, [UC Davis](https://piet.ucdavis.edu/zne2018sleepingbuildings); US data).
2. **Primary user:** A school or college eco-club student team, hostel warden or small-office admin. It works as a hands-on *learning tool*.
3. **Why it matters:** Every avoided kWh avoids ~0.71 kg CO₂ (FACT, via 03: [CEA v22](https://cea.nic.in/wp-content/uploads/baseline/2026/09/User_Guide__Version_22.0.pdf)). Eco-clubs exist at national scale (FACT, [DoSEL](https://dsel.education.gov.in/sites/default/files/update/Ek_Ped_Maa_Ke_naam_2-0.pdf)) but run awareness campaigns (INFERENCE).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Energy audits by BEE-certified auditors, which are costly and occasional (FACT, [Glocert](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/naac-green-energy-environment-audits-criterion-7/)).
   - Smart meters with utility apps (KNOWN; partial rollout).
   - "Switch off" posters.
   - None measures the after-hours base load in a way that can be verified at no cost (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** Base load = ΔkWh ÷ Δhours across a closed period. Two meter readings (closing and opening) measure it exactly, with no sensors. A second night after a "switch-off walk" verifies the saving (INFERENCE; standard energy-audit practice, KNOWN).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Photos:** reminders prompt a meter photo at closing and at opening.
   - **Reading:** Bedrock vision extracts the kWh register and meter serial, with a consistency check and a fallback to manual entry.
   - **Compare:** average after-hours kW against an essential-load estimate from the user's list (fridge, server, security lights) using a typical-watts table.
   - **Translate:** "phantom" kW into ₹ a year (tariff input) and kg CO₂.
   - **Act and verify:** a room-by-room switch-off checklist, then a re-measure night that gives a **verified reduction**.
   - **Compare across schools:** an eco-club leaderboard normalised by floor area.
7. **What's distinctive:** Sensorless, verifiable measurement of after-hours load. Students act as auditors, and the result is re-measured.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It creates a measurement protocol (paired readings plus a verification night) that didn't exist. AI only reads the meter.
9. **Data:** User photos (real meters); state tariff (user input); CEA emission factor (FACT).
10. **Architecture:** PWA → API Gateway → Lambda. S3 (photos), Bedrock vision (meter OCR), DynamoDB, EventBridge Scheduler (reminders), SES.
11. **AWS use:** Bedrock vision reading Indian meter LCDs. EventBridge drives the protocol. Modest but real.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** One building: baseline night → checklist → verification night → savings card.
13. **Demo plan:** A **real** measurement on a teammate's home or hostel meter over two nights (Fri and Sat). The data is real; no institution is needed.
14. **Strongest video moment:** Two meter photos become "your hostel block burns 3.4 kW all night = ₹1.9 lakh/yr = 21 t CO₂". After the switch-off walk the second night reads 1.6 kW, stamped **verified −53%** (numbers from the real test).
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The US base-load share is FACT (a proxy).
    - The team's own two-night result will be real.
    - National scaling is speculative.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** LCD meters cycle their displays, and Bedrock may misread digits.
    - **Product:** the meter may be locked or shared across buildings.
    - **Competition:** low-medium (carbon trackers are crowded; this is not one).
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** "Energy-saving tips plus a tracker" is common (05).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** A paired-reading protocol that yields an after-hours kW and a **re-measured** saving. Combine with C-12 as "measure tonight, prove it on the bill."

#### C-12 · ATR Proof: weather-normalised proof that a campus saved energy (Waste & Energy, cross Heat)

1. **Actual problem:** Colleges commission green and energy audits for NAAC Criterion 7.1. Action-Taken Reports must show improvement (FACT, [Glocert](https://www.glocertinternational.com/resources/guides/naac-green-energy-environment-audits-criterion-7/)). But comparing year-on-year bills is distorted by hotter summers and calendar shifts, so "savings" are unprovable or illusory (INFERENCE).
2. **Primary user:** A college's IQAC coordinator or estate officer. Also school administrators and office facility managers.
3. **Why it matters:** Accreditation pressure creates recurring audits nationwide (INFERENCE from NAAC's scope). Unverified claims undermine real efficiency investment (INFERENCE).
4. **Current workaround and alternatives:**
   - Consultancy audit reports (FACT, Glocert).
   - Utility-bill dashboards such as EnergyCAP abroad (FACT: [EnergyCAP on IPMVP](https://www.energycap.com/?p=1910)). These are commercial and not Indian.
   - No free tool combines bill extraction, cooling degree days and Indian campus calendars (INFERENCE).
5. **Underlying insight:** International measurement-and-verification practice (IPMVP Option C) proves whole-facility savings by adjusting the baseline to current weather and conditions (FACT, EnergyCAP). Indian campuses never do this; their cooling load swings with summer heat, so normalisation changes conclusions (INFERENCE).
6. **Mechanism:**
   - **Extract:** upload 12–36 monthly electricity bills; Textract or Bedrock extracts units, billing period and sanctioned load.
   - **Weather:** daily temperature for the location from Open-Meteo, giving cooling degree days per billing period.
   - **Baseline:** fit kWh = a·days + b·CDD + c·(vacation-month flag).
   - **Verify:** after the intervention date, adjusted baseline minus actual gives savings with an uncertainty band, in kWh, ₹ and tCO₂.
   - **Flag anomalies:** any bill more than 2σ above prediction.
   - **Report:** an ATR draft with charts.
7. **What's distinctive:** Formal M&V normalisation applied to Indian campus bills, producing accreditation-ready evidence.
8. **Not just a new UI?** It uses statistical baseline modelling, not bill display.
9. **Data:**
   - User bills: PDFs or photos.
   - Open-Meteo historical temperature (docs FACT; live call not verified).
   - CEA emission factor (FACT).
10. **Architecture:** Step Functions pipeline (Textract → Lambda normaliser → Lambda regression → Bedrock report). S3, DynamoDB, Amplify.
11. **AWS use:** Textract and Bedrock for messy bills. Step Functions makes the pipeline visible. Moderate-strong.
12. **Smallest useful MVP:** 24 bills, one intervention date, and a verified-savings chart plus an ATR paragraph.
13. **Demo plan:** A teammate's real household or hostel bills (24 months; real), with an intervention date such as an AC replacement or a labelled hypothetical. Weather is real. No institution needed.
14. **Strongest video moment:** A naive comparison says "+6% usage, the retrofit failed". The weather-normalised view says "summer 2026 had 18% more cooling degree days; adjusted, you **saved 9% ± 3%**". The conclusion flips on screen.
15. **Expected-impact evidence:**
    - The method is established internationally (FACT, EnergyCAP).
    - Its effect on Indian campus decisions is HYPOTHESIS.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** a 12-point regression is fragile; Indian bill formats vary.
    - **Product:** the boring "compliance" framing.
    - **Competition:** low.
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** Bill-reading with an LLM is easy, so "AI reads your electricity bill" will be common (INFERENCE).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** Weather- and calendar-normalised savings with uncertainty, producing an ATR. **Pairs naturally with C-11.**

#### C-13 · PoolDrop: all-or-nothing pooled e-waste pickups (Waste & Energy)

   - Producer take-back schemes (KNOWN).
   - One-off RWA drives (KNOWN).

9. **Data:** User pledges; the CPCB authorised list (PDF exists; [NDMC](https://www.ndmc.gov.in/pdf/cpcb_approved_list_of_e-waste_recyclers_dismantler.pdf); not opened); recycler minimums (gap).

13. **Demo plan:** Teammates pledge real items on phones. The recycler booking is a **mock** (labelled).
14. **Strongest video moment:** The 10th pledge pushes the bar past 50 kg, and every phone buzzes at once: "Pickup locked Sun 10:00, Gate 2".
15. **Expected-impact evidence:** The informal share is FACT. Pooling raising the formal share is HYPOTHESIS.
16. **Risks:**
    - **Technical:** low.
    - **Product:** no real recycler partner this weekend; the threshold value is a guess.
    - **Competition:** medium (e-waste locators; 05: 3/100).
17. **Why another team would build something similar:** E-waste pickup is a known genre (05).
18. **What would distinguish ours:** All-or-nothing pooling with pool merging. A thin moat.

#### Shortlist and combinations

1. **C-05 Heat Roll-Call (Heat & Water).** It meets every constraint, has an emotionally legible demo and makes Step Functions the protagonist on screen.
   - **Main weakness:** it is October. Mitigate by replaying a real May 2024 heat day, labelled, and framing it as "ready before March 2027".
   - **KPI:** share of registered vulnerable people contacted within 2 h. Do not claim lives saved.
2. **C-08 Monsoon Replay (Heat & Water).** Real AWS Open Data and real rainfall, end to end, with a numeric "aha" (litres → tankers → ₹).
   - **Breadth:** every Indian city with an RWH mandate.
   - **Check before choosing:** Open-Meteo archive access from a laptop, and Overture footprint density for a Bengaluru test polygon.
3. **C-06 ShadeSite (+ C-07 module) (Heat & Water).** The most distinctive mechanism and the best visual. It uses AWS-hosted canopy data, verified accessible.
   - **Highest build risk** (GIS); scope ruthlessly to one campus and one sun window.
   - Conflicts with C-05 and C-08 for the Heat & Water slot. **Pick one per team.**
4. **C-11 + C-12 "Measure tonight, prove it on the bill" (Waste & Energy).** Real self-collected data, high buildability and a verification loop that judges rarely see.
   - Waste & Energy is the less crowded track (05).
5. **C-02 ReCheck (Waste & Energy).** Turns a crowded genre into a new metric. Go/no-go depends on the ICMyC CSV having closure timestamps.

**C-10 FestLoop** is the reserve: very relatable to student judges, but it needs defensible coefficients.

**Where the lens clusters.** This lens produces strong **Heat & Water** concepts (C-05, C-06, C-08), and Heat & Water is likely ~35% of the field (05). Its strong **Waste & Energy** concepts (C-11/12, C-02, C-10) face a weaker median field (05). **Air concepts here are weaker or NCR-only** (C-03, C-04), consistent with 05's warning that Air is the most crowded track.

#### Cross-cutting design choices (INFERENCE)

- **Fixtures:** label any synthetic people or reports on screen and use real public data for everything else (rulebook R7).
- **No SMS in India.** Use SES email, web push or a Telegram bot.
- **Teammates as actors.** Use them in role-play (buddies, adopters, task owners) rather than outside people. That satisfies D without needing anyone else's cooperation.
- **A verification loop in every concept** (re-check, re-measure, reconcile, normalise). It is the lens's differentiator against "information-only" entries (05 §3, pattern 4).
